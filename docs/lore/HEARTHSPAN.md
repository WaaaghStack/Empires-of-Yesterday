# The Hearthspan

Before there were planets, there was **Hearth**: one world with too many skies. It did not explode. It **aged unevenly**. Regions that had already happened pulled away from regions that had not, until the globe tore along the grain of its own history.

What remains is a belt of worlds — **the Hearthspan** — still close enough that you can smell each other's weather, far enough that a life can be spent in transit.

## The Wake

Between the shards runs **the Wake**: not a tunnel, not a god, not a sea. It is leftover **Yesterday** — mass that remembers being one planet.

Ships do not jump. They **owe** the distance to the past and pay it as they go. The payment is called **After**. Spend After and you arrive. Spend too much in one corridor and the Wake thins. Spend it all and you get an **Open Tear**: a place where cause arrives without effect, and armies march into days that will not keep them.

This is why the title is literal. **Empires of Yesterday** are polities that harvest residual time-mass to stay warm in a cooling present.

## Travel

Interplanetary travel is ordinary and expensive. Surface empires treat it like a winter: planned, resented, survived. Wakeborn treat it like a room.

The live game's single globe is **one shard** — a provincial war. The rest of the span files it under weather.

## Tendency (design law for this bible)

No species is the correct playstyle. Directness is a culture, not a virtue. Indirectness is a culture, not a cheat. Every people has a way to **end the setting** that feels like *them* winning, not like a reskin of total conquest.

Live World Conquest locks (F5 land/zero-pressure, soldiers + bombers, R1, A14, …) still govern **the shard you can play today**. They are not the Hearthspan's win table.
