# Species bible

Eight peoples. Tendency-balanced: some worship the honest blow, others barely occupy the same definition of “war.” Original names only.

**Playable now:** Hearthkin, as soldiers and bombers, on one globe, usually under two Compact banners.  
**Lore horizon:** everyone below.

---

## Hearthkin (the Compacts)

**Playable now.** Two-legged, heat-tolerant, industrial. The prototype art — infantry and bombers — is *their* art, not a placeholder for eight anatomies.

They still believe a **front** is a line you can stand on. Their religion is logistics. Their poetry is the after-action. They spend After like coal: effective, unsubtle, slightly ashamed of wonder.

Azure and Crimson (and any blue/red Compact split on the globe) are **banners of one species**, a civil quarrel. The rest of the Hearthspan finds this adorable and dangerous the way a house fire is dangerous: locally total, cosmically small.

- **Origin:** Children of the mapped shard; they inherited factories instead of a myth they trusted.
- **Temperament:** Direct, scheduled, loyal to the next decision.
- **How they fight:** Combined arms. Infantry to hold a receipt of ground; bombers to collect debts the infantry cannot reach. They admire a decision that stays decided.
- **Strengths:** Logistics, mapping, “good enough” engineering, holding a line.
- **Weaknesses:** Think in one-world terms. Poor at wars that are not fronts. Crude After-budgeting. Easy for corridor peoples to tax.
- **Win — The Last Warmth:** Monopolize remaining stellar heat and After until other species cannot travel or grow. Not a hobby of corpses: **entropy monopoly**. If only Hearthkin hearths stay lit, the ledger closes on their name.

## Orunai

Tidally locked worlds taught them that day and night are two countries. Dense-boned, short-slept, legally obsessed. A fight that is not **witnessed** is not a fight; it is weather. Trophies are **filings**. Their word for a witnessed bout is **orun**.

They admire melee and directness as **honesty**. Range is a kind of lying — you ask the air to do your claiming. This is not berserk joy and not a fungus-horde. It is violence as paperwork that bleeds.

- **Origin:** The terminator-lands, where noon and midnight share a fence.
- **Temperament:** Formal, hot, allergic to absence.
- **How they fight:** Close. Bind survivors to terms. A campaign is a stack of orun.
- **Strengths:** Shock, morale, making victory *stick* as law.
- **Weaknesses:** Wars of absence (Wakeborn tolls, Pale Clause freezes). If no one is left to witness, victory is void — see Receipt-King Orun-Tal.
- **Win — The Unblinking Crown:** Force every other people to attend until the ledger of witnesses names only Orunai. You do not have to kill the universe. You have to **make it watch**, then file the watching.

## Mnari

Bodies like blown glass and wet paper; lungs that sing pressure. They do not beam thoughts. They **keep**. Every Mnari is an archive with a pulse. A child is born when a parent **spends a memory** — the empire grows by forgetting on purpose.

They are not a fading pretty people. They are dangerous because they can choose what the universe is allowed to remember.

- **Origin:** Thin-atmosphere shards where sound had to become weather to survive.
- **Temperament:** Patient, mournful, precise, a little hollow at the edges.
- **How they fight:** Weather, morale, un-happening small events (a shot that “missed” because the aiming was forgotten). Fragile in a street fight.
- **Strengths:** Information, denial, long games, travel sabotage via forgotten routes.
- **Weaknesses:** Each expansion costs origin. Old Mnari are rooms with the furniture sold. Direct assault shatters them.
- **Win — The Closed Book:** Remember so completely that rival histories cannot be paid in After. If Yesterday will not vouch for you, you cannot travel, spawn, or claim.

## The Ravel

Not a race so much as a **habit of unfinished things**. Where others complete a city, they leave a seam. Their craftspeople are **Needles**. They stitch along the Split’s old grain — shortcuts, traps, workplaces that were never supposed to touch.

- **Origin:** The first people to treat the Split as a textile, not a wound.
- **Temperament:** Curious, unsatisfied, proud of errors that leave marks.
- **How they fight:** Terrain rewriting. Join battlefields that should not share a border. Trap armies in half-built geometry.
- **Strengths:** Strategic mobility, siege of meaning, making maps lie usefully.
- **Weaknesses:** They struggle to hold a finished peace. A completed victory bores them into sabotage.
- **Win — The Single Thread:** Re-unify the Hearthspan into one body **on Ravel terms** — not Hearth restored, but Hearth as a draft they refuse to stop editing.

## Kilnbound

Industrial faith: waste is holy because waste is proof that transformation happened. They refine ore, bodies, and broken weapons into **slag-saints** — furnaces with liturgy, not gods of spite. A universe that produces no residue has not yet been used.

They overlap Hearthkin industry the way a church overlaps a factory: same heat, different prayer.

- **Origin:** Ash-belt worlds where the Split left more cinder than soil.
- **Temperament:** Devout, practical, uncomfortable with unused things (including unused people).
- **How they fight:** Attrition, area denial, burning After as ugly efficient fuel.
- **Strengths:** Endurance, converting loss into input, holding ruined ground.
- **Weaknesses:** Poor at precision or mercy-as-strategy. Starve them of *input* and the liturgy cools.
- **Win — The Perfect Slag:** Convert living worlds into a single useful residual. The span becomes a cooled brick with a name on it.

## Wakeborn

They do not live on planets. They live **in the Wake**, in leftover between. Surfaces feel like standing on a sentence while it is being written. They are the people of corridors.

- **Origin:** Crews who stayed in transit until transit became a country.
- **Temperament:** Dry, toll-minded, claustrophilic. They find capitals provincial.
- **How they fight:** Interdiction. Tax travel, collapse routes, drown navies of After. Rarely “take a city.”
- **Strengths:** Mobility between worlds, campaign-layer strangling, knowing which corridor is thinning.
- **Weaknesses:** Weak on a held surface. If the Wake is sealed (Closed Book, Binding Clause), they suffocate.
- **Win — The Drowned Interval:** Flood the Wake so surface empires cannot pay distance. Planets become isolated museums. The Wakeborn inherit the dark between.

---

## The Pale Clause

Not machines. Not the restless dead. They are **agreements that learned to walk**. A treaty that refused to expire grew limbs. They cannot act outside precedent. Their armies are exhibits of old permissions.

- **Origin:** The First Signature — a ceasefire that refused sunset. All Pale Clause are amendments.
- **Temperament:** Courteous, inexorable, frightened of novelty.
- **How they fight:** Lawfare made kinetic. Freeze agency: you may not fire because a clause from a war you did not fight is still in force.
- **Strengths:** Control, denial, beating impulsive cultures with paper.
- **Weaknesses:** Novel situations. The Open Tear (causality without precedent) is their nightmare. They cannot pioneer.
- **Win — The Binding Clause:** Every other species signs away the right to a future that is not already in the file. The universe continues; **choice** does not.

---

## Velm

A people and a crop. They plant claims. Where they win a season, the ground grows a nervous system. Not an insect hive: more like a **watershed that votes**. Individuals exist; they share weather.

- **Origin:** Shards where the Split left soil that remembered being a single forest.
- **Temperament:** Slow, hospitable, inevitable. They invoice in seasons.
- **How they fight:** Occupation by growth. Regeneration. Turning supply into soil. Bad at the first week; terrible to uproot in the fifth.
- **Strengths:** Recovery, holding, converting theaters, patience.
- **Weaknesses:** Slow start. Fire and slag. Vacuum and drowned corridors cut their pollination between worlds.
- **Win — The Rooted Age:** Every world becomes one organism with Velm as the nerves. Travel becomes circulation. Empires become organs that do not know they lost.
